# Handover: the enhancer session, 2026-08-09 to 08-10

Written for whoever picks this up cold. Everything below was measured in this
session; where a claim was later disproved, both the claim and the correction
are here, because the corrections are the useful part.

Live at publish 93. Four stores green throughout.

---

## The big change: DeepFilterNet at the head of the capture chain

`core/src/audio/deepfilter.rs`, running after the raw taps and before
everything else.

**Why it was adopted.** With music playing, the gaps between words sit **1.5 dB**
below the speech, so no threshold on level can separate them — which is why six
hand-built features in `MUSIC_GATE.md` all failed. DeepFilterNet takes 19.0 dB
out of the gaps and 4.5 dB out of the speech: **1.5 dB becomes 16.0 dB**.

**Three things decided the shape, and none was in the plan.**

- **The low-latency model has `lookahead 0`.** The standard DFN3 needs two
  frames and would cost 10 ms. It is also the only one that loads: the standard
  model fails inside tract 0.21.4 with `duplicate name /convt3/Conv.bias`.
- **The geometry is identical to ours** — 48 kHz, hop 480, FFT 960. A capture
  block *is* one model frame; nothing is buffered or resampled.
- **It cross-compiles to all five targets** because `tract` is pure Rust. This
  is why it lives in `core` and the YAMNet classifier does not.

**tract is pinned to `=0.21.4` in `core/Cargo.toml`.** `deep_filter` asks for
`^0.21.4`; 0.21.14 removed `Graph::symbol_table` and the crate does not compile
against it. A pin that lives only in a lock file is one `cargo update` from
gone.

**It steps down rather than stuttering**: 100 consecutive frames over 10 ms and
it gives up a rung — full, reduced, ERB-only, bypassed. It used to go straight
from everything to nothing, which is what a low-end Android got. See `Effort`
in `deepfilter.rs` and the rung table there; every step was measured on the
phone that reported the fault.

## Three faults found by testing, and what each turned out to be

### 1. Recordings were of enhanced audio, not the microphone

The enhancer ran *before* the recorder took its copy, and the comment beside it
claimed the opposite. Every number in `MUSIC_GATE.md` comes from `.s16` files on
the assumption that they are the microphone, and audio carries no record of what
captured it — so this would have invalidated the corpus with no symptom at all.
Exactly the failure `core/src/audio/record.rs` exists to prevent, and it has
happened to this project once before.

Fixed by moving the enhancer below the `raw` clone. **Two tests**, because one
alone is satisfied by an enhancer that does nothing: the ordering, and that the
enhancer measurably changes a block.

The classifier stays on the raw side deliberately — it looks for the background
and the enhancer removes the background; fed the enhancer's output it would
never hear music again.

### 2. The app crashed sometimes on Android

Not the enhancer. The tombstone:

```
Fatal signal 11 (SIGSEGV), Cause: null pointer dereference
#02 TfLiteInterpreterAllocateTensors+8  libtensorflowlite_jni.so
```

The **YAMNet classifier's GPU delegate**, segfaulting on an OPPO A3s (Adreno
506). Native, so the Dart `catch` never ran. Removed on Android; it was buying a
31-of-47 partial offload for a model that runs once every two seconds.

### 3b. Nothing was watching the input, and it cost an evening

**The meter beside the gain slider reads `analysis.level_db`, which is measured
after RNNoise and the profile filters.** It can sit well below full scale while
the microphone input is pinned at it — so "the meter never reaches 100%" and
"35% of recorded samples are at full scale" were both true, of two different
signals, and the argument between them went round twice.

`record.rs` compounds it: `s.clamp(-1.0, 1.0)` on the way to i16, so a
recording of an overdriven input comes back visibly clipped with nothing to say
whether the clipping happened before the chain or inside it.

Fixed in two parts, build 88 and 89:

- **A Microphone peak counter** in Diagnostics — the loudest raw sample in
  dBFS and a count of samples at full scale, measured on the same block the
  recorder clones. Red at the ceiling, cleared by the panel's Reset.
- **The level meter now reads the microphone.** The bar beside the gain slider
  takes the RMS of the raw block instead of `analysis.level_db`. The one
  control that sets the input level was showing the output of the thing that
  level feeds.

The post-suppression level stays in `ChainStatus` beside the floor and the
activation threshold, and the panel's gate trio now reads all three from there
— they are only meaningful against each other. The row is renamed *After
suppression* to say what it is.

**Resolved on the next recording, and the instrument is what resolved it.**
`20260810-1006-000`, 118 s of voice over music from build 89:

| clip | build | peak | at full scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `20260810-0131-000` | 87 | 1.000 | 8.67% |
| `20260810-0205-000` | 87 | 1.000 | 35.21% |
| **`20260810-1006-000`** | **89** | **0.621** | **0.00%** |

The first recording in the corpus that never reaches full scale, and the rider
reports it sounds much better.

**Nothing in the capture path changed between 87 and 89** — build 88 added the
peak counter and 89 moved the level meter, both measurement only. So the fault
was on the input side of a chain nobody could see the input of, and it went
away as soon as the meter started showing it. That is as close to naming the
gain slider as the evidence gets: strong, and inferred rather than observed,
because no build recorded what the slider was set to.

**Worth closing properly**: log the input gain in the `.csv` header. It is one
line, and it would have turned a session of argument into one column.

### 3. "Speech distorted when Auto switches to Helmet" — cause still open

**It is not Auto and not Helmet.** The recording is clipped, and identically
either side of the switch: **9.614% of samples at full scale before 27 s,
9.600% after**. No collapse below 180 Hz either, so the rumble filter is not it.
Helmet is simply the profile that makes already-clipped speech most obvious.

**I then blamed Apple's AGC, and that was wrong.** The `mode: .voiceChat`
experiment looked like the culprit — clipping rose from 0.27% to 9.61% when it
went in — so it was reverted. But build 87 *has* the revert and the next
recording was **35.2%** clipped, worse than before. The correlation was real and
the causation was not.

What misled me: once a third of samples are pinned at full scale, speech and the
gaps between words both *measure* as full scale, so the speech-to-gap separation
collapses to zero. I read that collapse as evidence of compression. It is a
consequence of the clipping, not a signature of its cause — the measurement had
already been destroyed by the thing I was trying to measure.

**Ruled out:** Auto, Helmet, the 180 Hz filter, the enhancer (the recorder is
above it since build 85), the mono downmix (it averages, correctly), and
`.voiceChat`. **Not ruled out:** the gain slider — the rider judged it by the
meter, which cannot show this (see 3b) — and an acoustically overdriven
microphone.

## Measurements worth not repeating

**Stage skipping has nothing left to give.** On a ride with no speech, 97.3% of
frames already run no decoder. Per-frame cost, release, desktop x86:

| Clip | mean | p99 | worst | zero-mask | both decoders |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ride, engine and wind | 0.54 ms | 2.75 | 4.05 | 97.3% | 2.6% |
| voice over music | 2.28 ms | 6.94 | **14.38** | 58.8% | 41.2% |
| ride, quiet, talking | 2.32 ms | 7.13 | **12.94** | 31.9% | 39.0% |

**The worst frame already exceeds the 10 ms budget on a desktop.** The real
requirement is a mean well under 10 ms; the guard measures the right thing.

**The zero-mask branch does not attenuate — it zeroes.** So a ride is 97% of
frames muted outright with a hard edge at every boundary. `MIN_DB` decides it and
is still the crate's default of −10 dB while a real ride sits at −15 dB. **This
is the leading untested candidate for any remaining choppiness** and the next
thing to try.

**Why music still passes the gate**, from the decision log of a real clip:
`level_db` separates transmitted from not at AUC 0.899 — the levels really are
far apart. But the gate is `vad && snr` plus a **300 ms tail** (200 ms hold,
100 ms fade), and **39% of transmitted blocks had VAD below 0.5**. They went out
on the hangover. `speaking` 42.1% against `transmitting` 52.6% is that gap. With
music underneath, every held-open window is full of music.

**LibriSpeech confirms the classifier's negative side**: 40 speakers, 342 frames
of clean speech, **0.0% fire at the shipped 0.30 bar**.

**Platform asymmetry, real and unsolved.** Android's capture goes through cpal's
AAudio backend, which sets **no input preset**, so AAudio applies its default
`VOICE_RECOGNITION` and Google's noise suppression runs before our chain. iOS
gets a raw microphone. The two platforms have never been doing the same work.
`.voiceChat` was the wrong answer; the question stands.

## The first clean measurement of the gate

With an unclipped input, `20260810-1006-000` is the first ride the decision log
can be read straight off. **These numbers supersede every gate measurement
above**, which were all taken on clipped audio:

- **transmitting 34.6%**, speaking 28.6%, gate open 29.2%.
- **27.4% of transmitted blocks had VAD below 0.5** — down from the 39% measured
  on clipped audio, but still better than a quarter of what goes out riding the
  300 ms tail rather than a live decision. With music underneath, that is what
  music leaks through.
- **Speech to silence, post-suppression, is 24.9 dB** (−41.1 against −66.0).
  Raw, before the enhancer, the same clip separates by **1.8 dB** — which is the
  1.5 dB that started all of this, and the chain really does turn one into the
  other.
- `level_db` separates transmitted from not at **AUC 0.878**; harmonicity at
  **0.564**, which is barely better than a coin toss and worth remembering next
  time a harmonicity gate is proposed.

## Where a capture block's 10 ms actually goes

Nothing measured this before, which is why the enhancer's own stopwatch was
able to convict it. Three stages matter and the rest round to zero — desktop
release, Helmet profile, over `20260810-1006-000`:

| Stage | mean | worst | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **enhancer** | 1.186 ms | 8.664 | **64.3%** |
| **encode** (Opus) | 0.387 ms | 3.020 | 21.0% |
| **suppression** | 0.248 ms | 2.076 | 13.4% |
| feedback | 0.023 ms | 3.624 | 1.2% |
| input, de-hiss, transmit | ~0 | — | ~0% |
| **whole block** | **1.846 ms** | **11.638** | |

**The enhancer is two thirds of the chain, and the other two thirds of what is
left cannot be reduced** — Opus is Opus, and suppression is the feature. So the
only lever on block cost is the enhancer, which is what the `Effort` ladder now
pulls.

## The same table, on the phone

Measured, not extrapolated: `chain_cost` cross-compiled to `aarch64-linux-android`
and run on the OPPO A3s over the same clip.

| Stage | mean | worst | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **enhancer** | 6.735 ms | 10.067 | **88.3%** |
| suppression | 0.366 ms | 0.488 | 4.8% |
| encode (Opus) | 0.348 ms | 1.027 | 4.6% |
| feedback | 0.178 ms | 0.665 | 2.3% |
| input, de-hiss, transmit | ~0 | — | ~0% |
| **whole block** | **7.632 ms** | **11.610** | **76.3% of budget** |

**Three things, and two of them correct an earlier claim here.**

1. **The enhancer is 88.3% of the block on ARM, against 64.3% on x86.** The
   caveat that this share might not transfer was right, and right about the
   direction: Opus and RNNoise carry hand-written NEON, `tract` does not, so
   everything except the enhancer shrinks relative to it. All seven other stages
   together are 0.9 ms.
2. **The extrapolation was wrong, and wrong the other way.** 6.23 ms ÷ 64.3%
   predicted a 9.7 ms block; the real figure is **7.632 ms**. Dividing by a
   share that was too small inflated the total — so the estimate was
   pessimistic, not optimistic as this file previously claimed. The reasoning
   about which way the error ran was as wrong as the number.
3. **The mean fits and the worst does not.** 7.632 ms against a 10 ms budget,
   with a worst block of **11.610 ms** — driven by a worst enhancer frame of
   10.067 ms, over budget on its own. And this is with nothing else running: no
   Flutter UI, no audio callbacks, no classifier. That is the whole account of
   the guard firing on this phone, and it needed no assumptions.

## The ladder, measured as blocks rather than as frames

`MW_EFFORT` on `chain_cost` picks the rung, so each one can be judged on what it
does to a whole block. On the OPPO, both clips, **mean / worst in ms**:

| Rung | voice over music | dense speech | worst inside 10 ms? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Full` | 4.30 / **13.36** | 7.86 / **13.24** | no |
| `Reduced` | 4.07 / **11.82** | 5.98 / **11.45** | no |
| `ErbOnly` | 3.71 / **8.12** | 5.36 / **8.37** | **yes** |
| `Bypassed` | 0.78 / 1.65 | 0.79 / 1.64 | yes |

**This corrects the sentence that used to be here**, which said `Reduced` put
the worst case back inside the budget. It does not, on either clip: 11.8 and
11.5 ms against a 10 ms deadline. Only `ErbOnly` does, and it does it on both.

The two columns disagree about the *mean* and agree about the *tail*, which is
the useful part. On dense speech `Reduced` is worth 24% of the mean (7.86 →
5.98) because most frames reach the DF decoder; on voice over music it is worth
5%, because 85% of frames are zero-masked and never reach it anyway. But the
worst block barely moves on either — the frames that run both decoders still
run both decoders, there are simply fewer of them. **Only the rung that stops
the DF decoder running at all touches the tail.**

So the ladder is right to have three steps rather than one, and right in its
order, but the useful mental model is not "three shades of less work":

- **`Reduced` buys average headroom**, which is what keeps a chain from
  overrunning on aggregate, and costs almost nothing in separation.
- **`ErbOnly` buys tail headroom**, which is what stops a single frame clicking
  — and is the only rung that actually meets the deadline on this phone.

A device that needs the tail fixed will walk to `ErbOnly` on its own, one
second later than if it had jumped. That second is the price of not spending
quality that a device with a merely-slow *mean* would have got away without.

**`core` does cross-compile to Android**, contrary to what this file said an
hour ago. `audiopus_sys` fails to configure inside the NDK's `flags.cmake`,
which reads as a toolchain conflict and is really policy **CMP0057**: the NDK
file uses `IN_LIST`, and Opus's `cmake_minimum_required` predates the policy
that makes `IN_LIST` an operator. `CLAUDE.local.md` has the shim and why setting
it in a toolchain file is too late.

Two rows in the panel exist to stop this being misread again:

- **Not in any stage** — the part of the block no stage was timing. These are
  wall clock, so a descheduled stage is charged for the wait; a large figure
  here means the worker is being interrupted, not that any stage is slow.
- **Waiting to be processed** — the capture backlog. Everything else is a cost;
  this is the consequence, and it climbs before anything is dropped.

`cargo test --release --test chain_cost -- --ignored --nocapture` with `MW_CLIP`
set reproduces the table offline, where nothing competes for the core. The
panel measures the same stages on the device, where things do. **Both are
needed**: the ratios here are the code, and the difference between the two is
contention.

## The GPU was the wrong lever, and the phone said so

**`docs/ENHANCER_GPU.md` has been rewritten around the measurements.** Both the
plan and its refutation are kept there, because the reasoning that led to the
GPU is the reasoning anybody else will arrive at.

The short version, in the order the evidence arrived:

1. **The graph cannot offload.** 483 nodes across three ONNX models; the
   compute is 6 GRUs, 22 Convs and 8 Einsums, and ~380 nodes are shape glue.
   Neither NNAPI nor the TFLite GPU delegate implements `GRU` or `Einsum`, so
   it would partition into islands with a copy at every boundary, a hundred
   times a second.
2. **There is no bandwidth to win.** `df_dec` is 19.2 MB of the 20 MB total —
   three GRUs of 512. At one frame per call that is matrix-by-*vector*, memory
   bound, and a mobile GPU shares the CPU's memory bus.
3. **The model already fits.** `tools/dfbench` cross-compiles the enhancer for
   `aarch64-linux-android` and runs it on the phone with **no APK at all**,
   which turns a Play round trip into a one-minute answer. On the OPPO A3s:
   mean **6.23 ms**, worst **9.27**, *zero* frames over the 10 ms budget.

What could not fit was the worst case. On the phone the whole block averages
7.632 ms and peaks at 11.610 — over budget — with the enhancer at 88.3% of it
and every other stage together under a millisecond. See the table above.

## Open, in the order I would take them

1. **Watch the ladder fire on the device.** Every rung is measured and the
   stepping is unit-tested, but no build with it has run on the phone yet.
   Publish 92 carries it; one ride with the panel open settles it.
2. ~~**The 300 ms hangover.**~~ — **measured, and I would leave it alone.**
   `tools/vad/hangover.py`, over the 10 voice-activated rides in the corpus
   (88 482 blocks, 376 s of airtime). Muted rides and the dead-microphone files
   are excluded: their envelope share is 0% for reasons that have nothing to do
   with the envelope.

   **First, a correction.** "27.4% of transmitted blocks ride it" is above, and
   it is the wrong quantity — that figure is transmitted blocks whose *VAD was
   below 0.5*, which is not the same as blocks the envelope sent. A block can
   sit below 0.5 and still be `speaking`, because the bar is per-profile.
   Measured properly — `transmitting` set with `speaking` clear, which is
   exactly a block that went out on the envelope — it is **17.0%**, 64 s of
   376 s. Per ride it runs 8.8% to 23.5%, and on the clip that produced the
   27.4%, 17.3%.

   **Why it should not be shortened.** The gaps between speech runs are
   strongly bimodal, and the hold is sitting in the valley:

   | p10 | p25 | p50 | p75 | p90 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 30 ms | 80 ms | **270 ms** | 1260 ms | 3140 ms |

   Half the gaps are under 270 ms — pauses inside a phrase — and the next
   quartile is already past a second, between utterances.

   **A gap closes at `hold + ONSET_LOOKAHEAD_MS`, not at the hold**, because
   the run after it opens the look-ahead early and the two envelopes meet that
   much sooner. A first version of this counted against the hold alone and put
   the shipped figure at 52.3%; it disagreed with the reconstruction below — 14
   gaps "newly split" where only 7 more runs appeared — which is what caught
   it. The shipped hold bridges to **490 ms**, which is **57.6%** of gaps: the
   whole within-phrase cluster and almost none of the long one.

   What shortening buys, reconstructed at each hold. `newly split` is gaps the
   shipped hold bridges and this one does not — each one a phrase coming apart
   — and it now agrees with the run count exactly, which is the cross-check:

   | Hold | Bridges to | Airtime | vs 330 | Runs | Newly split | Extra closures/min |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **330 ms** (ships) | 490 ms | 408 s | — | **147** | — | — |
   | 250 ms | 410 ms | 397 s | −2.9% | 154 | 7 | 1.1 |
   | 200 ms | 360 ms | 389 s | −4.8% | 159 | 12 | 1.9 |
   | 150 ms | 310 ms | 381 s | −6.8% | 167 | 20 | 3.2 |
   | 100 ms | 260 ms | 372 s | −8.9% | 175 | 28 | 4.5 |
   | 50 ms | 210 ms | 363 s | −11.1% | 190 | 43 | 7.1 |

   **The break is at 150 ms**, and it is visible in the table rather than
   argued: at 150 the envelope still bridges to 310 ms, above the 270 ms median
   gap. At 100 it bridges to 260 ms and has dropped *below* the median — it is
   now cutting into the main within-phrase cluster, and the damage accelerates
   from 20 splits to 28 and then 43.

   So 250 ms and 200 ms are not reckless: both still clear the median gap
   comfortably, at 7 and 12 split phrases across six and a half minutes of
   airtime. They are simply not worth it. The saving is 3–5% of an
   already-gated stream, nothing in this project is bandwidth-limited, and the
   loudest complaint all session has been words being cut.

   The music argument for shortening it does not survive either: the leak is an
   Auto-profile convergence transient (`docs/MUSIC_GATE.md`), and 17% of
   airtime held open is not where it lives.
3. ~~**`MIN_DB` tuning**~~ — **measured and closed.** Removing the zero-mask
   branch costs 3.4× the CPU (1.41 → 4.81 ms a frame) and 0.7 dB of separation
   (14.1 → 13.4). Worse on both axes, and worst on the devices already stepping
   down the ladder. The hard mute is what makes the gaps silent enough for the
   microphone's 1.8 dB to become 14. See `MIN_DB` in `deepfilter.rs`. A click
   at a zero-mask boundary is still invisible to this measure and needs an ear.
4. ~~**Log the input gain in the `.csv`**~~ — **done**, as `gain_db`. It was
   the last setting in the app that left no trace in the file.
5. **Profile re-tuning against the enhancer's output.** The profiles were tuned
   against a raw microphone and the premise has changed — and `Reduced`
   separating better than `Full` on voice over music says the enhancer's own
   settings are not tuned either.
6. **f16 weights**, if the ladder proves insufficient. Halves `df_dec`'s
   19.2 MB, but Cortex-A53 is ARMv8.0 with no half-precision *arithmetic*, so
   it could easily be slower there. Measure before believing, and note it needs
   `libDF` vendored.
7. **Windows classifier — parked.** Needs a CI job building
   `libtensorflowlite_c` from source; the package ships no DLL. macOS already
   works, so Windows is the only desktop without it, and the app degrades to no
   classifier rather than failing.

   Parked with a local build tree at `C:\src\tflite-build` that produced no DLL.
   Three things were learned, and they are the reason the next attempt should
   start from a clean directory rather than that one:

   - **`-DCMAKE_CXX_STANDARD=20` is silently ignored.** TFLite hardcodes
     `set(CMAKE_CXX_STANDARD 17)` in two of its own `CMakeLists.txt`, which win.
     Patching those two lines is what unblocks the compile.
   - **Read the whole build log.** `Select-Object -Last 25` hid the real error
     for an hour; the C++ standard failure was hundreds of lines above the tail.
   - **The Explorer dialogs during configure are Eigen, and harmless.**
     `eigen/CMakeLists.txt:584` does `include(CMakeDetermineFortranCompiler)`
     unconditionally, only to default `EIGEN_BUILD_BLAS`/`EIGEN_BUILD_LAPACK`.
     Under the **Visual Studio generator** that probe is a *project file*,
     `CompilerIdFortran.vfproj` — the Intel Visual Fortran format. With no Intel
     Fortran installed and no `.vfproj` association on the machine, launching it
     falls through to ShellExecute and Windows asks what to open it with, once
     per configure. Nothing is wrong: the answer is "no Fortran compiler", which
     is correct and wanted.

     **Configure with Ninja rather than Visual Studio** and it stops — the probe
     becomes an ordinary source compile that fails quietly. `-D`
     `CMAKE_Fortran_COMPILER=NOTFOUND` short-circuits it too. This is only ever
     a configure-time annoyance, so a parked build produces no more of them.
8. **iOS/macOS screenshots** for the site; and the sync screenshot, which only
   exists on the iOS simulator.

## The lesson that cost the most

**Every audio complaint in this session was diagnosed on a signal nobody could
see.** The panel had eleven numbers in it and not one of them was measured
before the chain ran, so an overdriven microphone presented as: speech
distortion, Helmet sounding worse than Standard, music surviving the gate, and
an argument between a meter and a measurement that were both correct about
different things.

Instrument the input first. A measurement taken after the thing you are
debugging cannot exonerate it.

## Two things that cost time and should not again

- **`\r` in a Windows path inside a Python heredoc** silently wrote a carriage
  return into a Rust doc comment: `bare CR not allowed in doc-comment`.
- **`cargo` and `flutter` are not on the default PATH**, and Git Bash's
  `/usr/bin/link` shadows MSVC's linker — Rust builds must run from PowerShell.
